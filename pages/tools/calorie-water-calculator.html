How many calories, and how much water

Enter your sex, age, weight, height and how active you are, choose a goal, and the calculator estimates three things: the energy your body burns at rest, the calories you burn in a typical day, and a daily target to eat for your goal. It also gives a water estimate based on your weight, exercise and climate. It is built for healthy adults over 18, and you can switch between metric and imperial units.

The maths behind it

Resting energy (BMR) comes from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is widely used and tends to be more accurate than older formulas. For women it is 10 x weight in kg + 6.25 x height in cm - 5 x age - 161, and for men the last term is +5. That number is then multiplied by an activity factor, from 1.2 for a desk job with little exercise up to 1.9 for hard training or a physical job.

Take a 30-year-old woman who weighs 70 kg, is 175 cm tall and exercises three to five days a week. Her BMR is 1,483 calories and her maintenance level, at a factor of 1.55, is about 2,298 calories a day. Choosing gradual weight loss takes 250 calories off that, which is roughly a quarter of a kilo a week, and faster loss takes off 500.

Where the water figure comes from

The water estimate starts with 35 ml per kilo of body weight, adds 350 ml for every 30 minutes of exercise, adds a little more in hot or humid weather, and rounds to the nearest 50 ml. For the same 70 kg person with 30 minutes of daily exercise in a temperate climate that is about 2.8 litres. It includes the water in tea, soup and food, so it is not all plain water, and it is a starting point rather than a prescription.

Use it as an estimate, then adjust

Every formula is an average. Two people with the same numbers can burn quite different amounts. The sensible way to use the result is to follow it for two or three weeks, watch what the scale and your energy do, and adjust by 100 to 200 calories. Very low targets are not a good idea without medical supervision. These figures do not apply to children, to pregnancy or breastfeeding, or to anyone with a medical condition that affects diet or fluid, so speak to a doctor or dietitian first in those cases. Nothing you enter is stored.

Questions people ask

How many calories do I need a day?

It depends on your age, sex, size and activity. Enter your details and the calculator estimates the amount to maintain your weight, and then adjusts it for your goal.

Which formula does the calculator use?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation for resting energy, multiplied by an activity factor.

How much weight will I lose with a 500-calorie deficit?

About half a kilo a week on average, though real results vary from person to person and week to week.

How much water should I drink?

The tool suggests about 35 ml per kilo of body weight plus extra for exercise and hot weather. Food and other drinks count toward it.

Is this medical advice?

No. It gives general estimates for healthy adults. Check with a doctor or dietitian if you have a health condition, are pregnant or want to lose weight quickly.
