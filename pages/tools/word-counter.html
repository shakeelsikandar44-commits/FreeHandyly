How to Use the Word & Character Counter on FreeHandyly

The FreeHandyly Word & Character Counter analyzes your writing as you type. There is nothing to install, no account to create, and no button to press to get results. Everything runs inside your browser, so your text stays on your device. Follow these steps to get accurate counts in seconds:

  1. Add your text. Type directly into the text box, paste from a document or email with the Paste button, or click Try sample text to see how the tool behaves.
  2. Read the live statistics. Words, characters (with and without spaces), sentences, paragraphs, average word length, estimated reading time and speaking time update with every keystroke.
  3. Check a character limit. Pick a preset such as a 60-character meta title, a 160-character meta description or a 280-character social post. The progress bar shows how much room you have left and turns red when you go over.
  4. Review keyword density. Choose single words, two-word phrases or three-word phrases. Leave “Ignore common words” ticked to hide filler such as “the” and “and” so the topics that matter rise to the top.
  5. Copy or clear. Use Copy text to move your final draft elsewhere, or Clear to start again.

How the numbers are calculated

A word is any run of letters or digits, so “well-known” and “don’t” each count as one word. Characters count every symbol including spaces, while the second figure excludes all whitespace. A sentence ends at a period, question mark, exclamation mark or ellipsis, and a paragraph is any non-empty line. Reading time assumes 225 words per minute, a common average for adults reading online, and speaking time assumes 130 words per minute, a comfortable pace for a presentation or voice-over.

Why This Tool is Helpful

Length rules appear everywhere in writing. Essays have minimums, search results truncate long titles, and social networks cut off posts. Checking length by hand is slow and error-prone, and a word processor does not tell you whether your meta description will fit in a search snippet or how often you repeated the same phrase. A single page that handles all of these checks saves time and prevents embarrassing mistakes.

  • Students can meet essay and assignment requirements without guessing, and spot overused words before a teacher does.
  • Bloggers and SEO writers can keep titles and descriptions within display limits, confirm that a target keyword appears naturally and avoid keyword stuffing.
  • Copywriters and marketers can trim ad headlines, email subject lines and captions to platform limits.
  • Speakers and video creators can turn a script into a timed talk using the speaking-time estimate.
  • Job seekers can keep cover letters and application answers within the requested length.
  • Translators and editors can quote projects by word count and verify deliverables quickly.

Because the analysis happens locally, the counter responds instantly even on long documents and works without sending private drafts to a server. That makes it safe for confidential writing such as contracts, unpublished manuscripts and client work.

Tips for writing to a target length

If you are over the limit, cut qualifiers such as “very” and “really”, replace long phrases with single words, and remove sentences that repeat an earlier point. If you are under, add a concrete example, a short definition or a brief summary instead of padding with filler. Re-check the keyword table after every major edit: if one phrase dominates, vary your wording; if your main topic is missing, work it into headings and opening sentences where it reads naturally.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How does the word counter count words?

It treats any run of letters or numbers, including hyphenated words and contractions, as one word. Extra spaces, tabs and line breaks are ignored, so the count matches what most word processors report.

Is my text saved or sent anywhere?

No. All analysis happens in your browser with JavaScript. Your text is never uploaded, stored or shared, and it disappears when you close or refresh the page.

What is a good keyword density?

There is no official target. Most SEO writers aim for a natural mention of the main phrase, often between 0.5% and 2%, and put readability first. Repeating a phrase unnaturally can hurt the reader experience.

How is reading time estimated?

Reading time divides your word count by 225 words per minute. Technical or dense text takes longer, so treat the result as a guide rather than a guarantee.

Does it work on mobile and with other languages?

Yes. The page is responsive, and counting uses Unicode letters, so accented and non-Latin words are supported. Languages written without spaces between words, such as Chinese or Japanese, are better measured by the character count.

How many words are on a page?

A single-spaced page holds roughly 500 words and a double-spaced page about 250, depending on font size and margins. Paste your text above to get an exact count instead of an estimate.

Does the character count include spaces?

The counter shows characters both with and without spaces, so you can match whichever rule a form, post or assignment uses.
