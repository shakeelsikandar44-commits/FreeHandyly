What the counter actually measures

Paste or type into the box and the numbers move as you go. Words are counted by splitting on spaces and line breaks. Characters are shown twice, with and without spaces, because some forms count one and some count the other. A sentence ends at a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark, and a new paragraph starts after a blank line.

Reading time assumes 225 words a minute and speaking time assumes 130. Those are ordinary averages for silent reading and for talking to an audience. Slow readers, technical text and nervous speakers will all run longer, so treat the figure as a planning estimate rather than a promise.

Limits that matter in practice

The character limit menu has a few presets because these are the ones people ask about most: a 60-character page title, a 160-character meta description, a 280-character post, a 2,200-character Instagram caption, and a custom box for essays or application forms. Pick one and the counter shows how far you are from the line.

One honest caveat on the SEO ones. Google cuts titles and descriptions by pixel width, not by a fixed number of characters, so a title full of capital W letters can be shortened at 55 characters while a row of lowercase i letters fits in 70. The presets are a good safety margin, not a guarantee.

Using the keyword table without overdoing it

Below the counter you will see the words and short phrases you use most. Switch between one, two or three word phrases, and tick the box that ignores very common words so that "the" and "and" do not crowd the list. This is useful for catching a habit, like writing "really" nine times in one page. It is not a target to hit. There is no ideal percentage that makes a page rank, and reading your draft out loud tells you more than any density figure.

A couple of ways people use it

A student with a 1,500-word limit pastes the draft, sees 1,642, and knows to cut about a hundred and forty words before submitting. A freelancer writing for a client who pays per word checks the final count before sending the invoice. Someone preparing a speech divides the speaking time by the slot they were given and trims accordingly. A marketer checks that an ad headline fits before uploading it, instead of finding out when the platform rejects it.

When your count will differ from another tool

Different programs disagree on edge cases such as hyphenated words, web addresses, numbers with commas and emoji. If a limit is strict, such as a visa form or an exam portal, leave a small margin instead of aiming for the exact number. Languages written without spaces between words, such as Chinese, Japanese and Thai, cannot be split into words this way, so rely on the character count for those.

Everything is calculated in your browser while you type. The text is not sent anywhere, so it is fine to paste drafts that are not public yet.

Questions people ask

How many words fit on one page?

It depends on font and spacing. A single-spaced page in a 12-point font holds roughly 500 words, and a double-spaced page holds about half that. For anything with a strict length, count the words rather than the pages.

Do spaces count as characters?

That depends on who set the limit. This tool shows both numbers, so you can check the one your form or platform uses.

What is a good keyword density?

There is no number that guarantees rankings. Use the table to spot words you repeat by accident or phrases that feel stuffed in, then write for the reader. If a sentence sounds odd read aloud, change it.

Is my text saved or uploaded?

No. The counting runs in your browser and nothing you type leaves your device.

Why is my word count different in Microsoft Word?

Word and other editors treat hyphens, slashes and symbols a little differently. The gap is usually a handful of words in a long document.
