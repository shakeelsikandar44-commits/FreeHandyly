How to Use the BMI Calculator on FreeHandyly

The FreeHandyly BMI Calculator estimates your body mass index from your height and weight in seconds. Choose metric or imperial units, pick the standard WHO categories or the lower South Asian cut-offs, and see your result on a colour-coded scale together with the healthy weight range for your height. Everything is calculated in your browser, so your measurements are never stored or sent anywhere.

  1. Choose your units. Select metric (kilograms and centimetres) or imperial (pounds, feet and inches). The input fields switch automatically.
  2. Enter your age. Add your age in years. This calculator is designed for adults aged 18 and over, because children and teenagers are assessed with growth charts instead of fixed categories.
  3. Enter height and weight. Type your height and your current weight. For the most accurate result, measure your height without shoes and weigh yourself in the morning in light clothing.
  4. Pick a reference. Standard WHO categories suit most adults. The South Asian reference uses lower cut-offs, because health risks such as type 2 diabetes tend to appear at a lower BMI in people of South Asian origin.
  5. Read your result. You see your BMI, your category, a marker on the colour scale and the weight range that matches the healthy BMI band for your height.

Why This Tool is Helpful

BMI is one of the quickest screening measures used by doctors, insurers and public health programmes. It turns height and weight into a single number that can be compared with standard ranges, which makes it easy to notice when a change in weight may be worth discussing with a professional. Calculating it by hand means squaring your height and dividing, which is easy to get wrong, especially when switching between pounds and kilograms.

  • People starting a health journey can see where they stand and discuss realistic goals with a doctor.
  • Fitness and sports enthusiasts can track weight changes alongside strength and fitness measures.
  • Families can check general ranges when supporting an adult relative.
  • Health coaches and students can demonstrate how height and weight relate to BMI categories.
  • Anyone preparing for a check-up can bring a quick reference number to an appointment.

How to interpret your BMI

BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. It does not measure body fat directly and cannot tell muscle from fat, so a muscular athlete can have a high BMI while carrying little excess fat. It also ignores where fat is stored, age, sex, ethnicity and medical conditions. Waist measurement, blood tests and a clinical assessment give a fuller picture. This calculator is for adults only and is not suitable during pregnancy. The result is information, not medical advice, and a number outside the healthy range is a reason to talk with a healthcare professional, not a reason to panic or to follow extreme diets.

Standard and South Asian categories

Under the standard WHO categories, a BMI below 18.5 is underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 is healthy, 25 to 29.9 is overweight and 30 or above is obese. Research on people of South Asian descent, including those from Pakistan, India and Bangladesh, shows a higher risk of diabetes and heart disease at lower BMI values, so many health bodies in the region treat 23 as the start of overweight and 25 as the start of obesity. If you are not sure which to choose, start with the standard scale and ask your doctor which thresholds they apply to you.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is considered healthy on the standard scale. With the South Asian reference the healthy range is 18.5 to 22.9.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI equals weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. In pounds and inches, multiply weight in pounds by 703 and divide by height in inches squared.

Is BMI accurate for athletes or older adults?

Less so. Muscle weighs more than fat, so athletes may score high, and older adults can have low muscle mass with a normal BMI. Use BMI together with waist size and professional advice.

Is my data saved?

No. The calculation happens in your browser, and nothing you enter is stored or sent to a server.

What is a normal BMI for women?

The WHO adult categories are the same for women and men: a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is classed as a normal weight range. BMI does not measure body fat directly, so treat it as a screening number.

What BMI cut-offs are used for South Asian adults?

Some guidelines use lower cut-offs for South Asian adults because health risks can appear at lower BMI values. You can switch to these cut-offs in the calculator.
