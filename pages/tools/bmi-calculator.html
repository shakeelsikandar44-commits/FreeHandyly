BMI is a screening measure for healthy adults, not a diagnosis. It cannot tell muscle from fat and does not apply during pregnancy. Speak to a doctor or dietitian about your health and weight.

What BMI is, and what it is not

Body mass index is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres, squared. A person who weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall has a BMI of 70 / (1.75 x 1.75), which is 22.9. Enter your age, weight and height in metric or imperial units and the calculator gives the number, the category it falls in, and the range of weights that would be considered healthy for your height.

Two sets of cut-offs

The standard WHO categories are underweight below 18.5, healthy from 18.5 to 24.9, overweight from 25 to 29.9 and obese at 30 or above. Research has shown that people of South Asian and some other Asian backgrounds can carry a higher health risk at lower BMI values, so many health bodies in the region use stricter cut-offs. You can switch to those: healthy is 18.5 to 22.9, overweight starts at 23 and obese at 25.

The difference is not small. Someone who weighs 82 kg and is 170 cm tall has a BMI of 28.4. On the standard scale that is overweight; on the South Asian scale it is in the obese range. Neither label is a diagnosis, but the second tells you that talking to a doctor about your numbers is worthwhile.

What BMI cannot see

BMI uses only height and weight, so it cannot tell muscle from fat. A strength athlete can land in the overweight range while having little body fat, and an older person who has lost muscle can look fine on paper while carrying too much fat. It says nothing about where fat is stored, and waist size is often a better warning sign. It is also not designed for children and teenagers, who are assessed against growth charts, or for pregnancy.

Using the result sensibly

Think of BMI as a first screen, the same way a thermometer is a first screen for fever. If your number is outside the healthy band, the next steps are things like measuring your waist, looking at blood pressure and blood sugar, and talking with a doctor or dietitian. If you want to turn the number into a plan, the calorie calculator on this site estimates daily needs for your goal. Your measurements stay in your browser and are not saved.

Questions people ask

How is BMI calculated?

Divide your weight in kilograms by your height in metres squared. In pounds and inches, multiply by 703 first.

What is a healthy BMI?

On the standard scale it is 18.5 to 24.9. Under the stricter South Asian reference it is 18.5 to 22.9.

Why are there two reference ranges?

Health risks such as type 2 diabetes begin at lower BMI values in South Asian populations, so lower cut-offs are often recommended there.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. BMI cannot separate muscle from fat, so very muscular people can show as overweight.

Can I use this for my child?

No. Children and teenagers are measured against age and sex growth charts. Ask your paediatrician.
