How to Use the Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator on FreeHandyly

The FreeHandyly Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator answers the question every freelancer asks: how much should I charge? Instead of copying a competitor's price, you start from the income you want and work backwards through taxes, business expenses, time off and the hours you can realistically bill. The result is a rate that covers your whole business.

  1. Enter your income goal. Type the yearly take-home income you want after tax, the amount you would like to actually keep.
  2. Add business expenses. Enter your monthly costs such as software, equipment, coworking, insurance, marketing and accounting.
  3. Set your tax rate. Enter an estimated percentage for income tax and self-employment contributions. Ask a local accountant if you are unsure.
  4. Describe your working time. Enter the hours you work per week, the percentage of that time you can bill to clients, and the weeks per year you take off for holidays, sickness and public holidays.
  5. Read your rate. The calculator shows the hourly rate to charge, the yearly and monthly revenue you need, your billable hours, and the effective rate on all hours worked. Add a buffer to cover late payments or slow months.

Why This Tool is Helpful

Many new freelancers set prices by comparing themselves to employees and forget that clients do not pay for admin, marketing, unpaid proposals, holidays or sick days. A salary of a given size does not translate to the same hourly figure for a freelancer, who needs a noticeably higher rate to end up with the same result. Knowing your minimum rate helps you negotiate with confidence and spot projects that do not pay enough.

  • New freelancers can set a first rate based on real costs rather than guesswork.
  • Designers, developers and writers can compare an hourly figure with project pricing.
  • Consultants and coaches can check that session prices cover preparation and admin time.
  • Employees moving to freelance can see what rate matches their current salary.
  • Agencies and virtual assistants can price packages and retainers sensibly.

Assumptions and how to use the result

The calculation treats business expenses as costs paid before tax and applies your tax rate to the profit, so revenue needed equals take-home income divided by one minus the tax rate, plus yearly expenses. Real tax rules vary by country and income level, so use the figure as a planning estimate. Treat the result as your minimum rate. Charge more for urgent work, specialised skills and clients who need ongoing support, and review your rate every six to twelve months. Many freelancers bill only half of their working hours, so be honest about the billable percentage.

Ways to raise your effective rate

If the number looks higher than the market pays in your field, adjust the inputs that you control before lowering your target. Raise your billable share by batching admin tasks, using templates for proposals and invoices, and saying no to unpaid work that does not lead anywhere. Reduce expenses by reviewing subscriptions every quarter. Specialise in a niche, because clients pay more for a clear outcome than for general availability. Switch some work to fixed project fees or monthly retainers so that finishing faster increases your earnings. Finally, request deposits and set clear payment terms so that late invoices do not force you to add a large buffer.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What percentage of my time is billable?

Many freelancers bill between 50 and 70 percent of their working hours, because the rest goes to admin, marketing, learning and finding clients. Start with 60 percent if you are unsure.

Should I include taxes in my rate?

Yes. Your rate has to cover the tax you will owe as well as the money you keep. Enter an estimated overall tax percentage, and consult a local tax professional for the exact figure.

Is hourly pricing better than project pricing?

Neither is always better. Use your hourly figure as a floor, estimate the hours for a project and price above that, so faster work does not reduce your income.

Which currency does it use?

Any. Pick a currency symbol for display. The calculator does not convert between currencies; use the currency converter for that.

How do I convert a yearly salary to an hourly rate?

Divide the annual amount by the hours you actually work. For example, 52 weeks x 40 hours is 2,080 hours, so 60,000 divided by 2,080 is about 28.85 per hour. Freelancers should also add taxes, expenses and unbilled time, which this calculator does for you.

How much should a beginner freelancer charge?

There is no single number. Start from your income goal and costs, compare what similar freelancers in your field and country charge, and raise your rate as your skills and demand grow.
