Why your hourly rate is higher than your salary divided by 2,080

An employee on $30 an hour earns for every hour at work. A freelancer does not. Some of your week goes to emails, invoices, proposals and clients who never reply, and you are not paid for any of it. You also cover your own software, equipment and taxes, and nobody pays you for holidays or sick days. If you quote the number you would like to earn per hour of work, you will end up earning much less than you planned.

This calculator works backwards from the money you want to take home. Enter your yearly take-home goal, monthly business expenses, tax rate, work hours per week, the share of your time that you can actually bill, weeks off per year and an optional safety buffer. It tells you the hourly rate that covers the lot.

The formula, with an example

First it grosses up your goal for tax by dividing by one minus the tax rate, then adds a year of expenses, then adds the buffer. Finally it divides by the hours you can really bill: hours per week, times the weeks you work, times the billable share.

Suppose you want $60,000 after tax, spend $500 a month on business costs, pay 20 percent tax, work 40 hours a week, take 4 weeks off, bill 60 percent of your time and add a 10 percent buffer. Revenue needed is ($60,000 / 0.8 + $6,000) x 1.10 = $89,100. Billable hours are 40 x 48 x 0.6 = 1,152. The rate is $77.34 an hour. The tool also shows a lower figure, about $46.41, which is what you would charge if every working hour were billable. The gap between the two numbers is the cost of admin time.

Choosing sensible inputs

For the tax rate, use an effective rate that includes any self-employment contributions, not the top bracket you saw online. For billable share, new freelancers often land between 50 and 70 percent; track a few weeks of real work if you are unsure. Put health cover, a pension contribution, a coworking desk and software subscriptions in the monthly expenses. The buffer is for slow months and late payers, and 10 percent is a modest cushion.

What the number does not tell you

It is the minimum you need, not what the market will pay. Compare it with what clients in your field and country actually accept. If the number is too high, the levers are fewer weeks off, lower expenses, more billable time, or moving to project pricing where you charge for the result instead of the hour. Treat the output as planning help, not tax or financial advice.

Questions people ask

How do I work out my freelance hourly rate?

Divide the income you need, grossed up for tax and expenses, by the hours you can realistically bill. The calculator does the arithmetic and shows the formula.

What is a billable percentage?

It is the share of your working hours that you can charge clients for. The remainder goes on admin, marketing and finding work. Many freelancers bill 50 to 70 percent of their time.

Should I include tax in my rate?

Yes. Enter your effective tax rate and the calculator raises your target so that the amount left after tax matches your goal.

What is the safety buffer for?

It covers quiet months, unpaid invoices and surprise costs. A buffer of around 10 percent is a reasonable start.

Can I use another currency?

Yes. Pick dollar, euro, pound, rupee, dirham or no symbol. The maths is the same in every currency.
