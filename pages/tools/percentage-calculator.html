One page for every percentage question

Percentage problems look the same on paper but ask different things, so this page has a separate small calculator for each. Pick the question that matches yours, type the two numbers, and the answer appears with the formula written out underneath. You can see how it was worked out and repeat it by hand if you want to check.

The panels cover: what is X percent of Y, X is what percent of Y, percentage increase or decrease from one value to another, add or subtract a percentage from a number, X is P percent of what number, simplifying a ratio, and a discount and sale price calculator with a second discount and tax.

Working out a sale price

Say a jacket is listed at $80 with 25 percent off, and the shop takes an extra 10 percent off at the till. Enter 80, 25 and 10 and the tool applies the first discount ($60), then the second ($54). Add 8 percent sales tax and the total comes to $58.32. Note that two discounts of 25 and 10 percent do not make 35 percent. The second one is taken from the already reduced price, so the real saving is 32.5 percent.

Increase, decrease and the 20 and 25 trap

If a price goes from 80 to 100, it has risen by 25 percent, because the 20 extra is a quarter of the original 80. If it then falls from 100 back to 80, that is a drop of only 20 percent, because the same 20 is now a fifth of 100. This catches people out in salary talks, stock prices and exam scores. The percentage change panel always measures from the starting value, so put the old number in the first box.

Working backwards

"X is P percent of what number" is for reversing a percentage. If a shirt costs $60 after a 25 percent discount, the original price is $80, not $75, because 60 is 75 percent of the original. The same panel helps when you know the tax-inclusive amount and need the price before tax, or when a total includes a tip and you want to split it fairly.

A note on percentage points

When a rate moves from 4 percent to 6 percent, it rose by two percentage points but by 50 percent in relative terms. News stories and loan offers use both, and they are very different numbers. Use the percentage change panel for relative change, and subtract directly for point difference.

Questions people ask

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For 15 percent of 200, that is 200 x 15 / 100 = 30. The first panel does it for you.

How do I find the percentage increase between two numbers?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. Going from 80 to 100 gives (100 - 80) / 80 x 100 = 25 percent.

Why do two discounts not add up?

The second discount is applied to the price after the first one, not to the original price, so 25 percent then 10 percent saves 32.5 percent in total.

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Use the "X is P percent of what number" panel. Enter the sale price and the percentage you paid, which is 100 minus the discount.

Is tax added before or after the discount?

This calculator applies discounts first and then adds tax to the reduced price, which is how most shops do it. Check your local rules if the difference matters.
