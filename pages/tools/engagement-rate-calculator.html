How to Use the Engagement Rate & Video Retention Calculator on FreeHandyly

The FreeHandyly Engagement Rate & Video Retention Calculator turns raw social numbers into two figures that brands and creators discuss every day: how strongly people interact with your content, and how much of each video they actually watch. Enter your numbers and read the result instantly, with the formula shown underneath.

  1. Choose how to measure. Select engagement by followers when you want to know how your audience responds, or by views when you want to judge a single video or Reel.
  2. Enter the interactions. Fill in likes, comments, shares and saves. Leave any you do not track empty. Add the number of posts if you are entering totals for several posts.
  3. Enter the base number. Type your follower count or the views the post received, depending on the mode you picked.
  4. Read the engagement rate. The result shows the rate as a percentage, the total interactions, the average per post and a plain-language rating based on common rules of thumb.
  5. Check video retention. In the second panel enter the video length and the average view duration from your analytics, using seconds or a mm:ss format. You get the percentage of the video viewers watch on average.

Why This Tool is Helpful

Follower counts alone say little about how valuable an account is. A smaller account with an active audience often delivers more attention than a large one that few people react to. Engagement rate and retention put different accounts and videos on the same scale, which helps with setting goals, spotting what works and discussing rates with sponsors using real numbers.

  • Influencers and creators can include a clear engagement figure in media kits and brand pitches.
  • Brands and agencies can compare creators fairly before choosing partners.
  • Social media managers can track whether new content formats improve interaction month by month.
  • YouTubers can see whether a longer video holds attention, using the retention figure.
  • Small businesses can judge whether their posts are resonating with followers or just collecting impressions.

How to read the numbers

There is no single official formula, so always state which one you used when you share a result. Engagement by followers is common for comparing accounts, while engagement by views is better for individual videos. As a rough rule of thumb, below 1 percent is low, 1 to 3 percent is average, 3 to 6 percent is good and above 6 percent is excellent, although typical rates differ a lot between platforms, niches and account sizes, and smaller accounts usually score higher. For retention, shorter videos naturally keep a higher share of viewers, so compare similar lengths with each other.

Common mistakes when comparing engagement

Comparing numbers without matching the method is the most common error. A rate calculated from followers cannot be compared with one calculated from views, and a single viral post can lift an average well above what a typical post achieves. Look at the last ten to twenty posts rather than the best one, and compare accounts of a similar size, because very large accounts almost always show lower rates than small, close-knit communities. Some platforms hide likes or count saves differently, so note which interactions you included. Remember also that purchased followers and automated comments inflate reach while lowering the rate, which is why brands increasingly look at engagement before reach.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the formula for engagement rate?

Add likes, comments, shares and saves, divide by followers (or views), then multiply by 100. If you enter several posts, the interactions are averaged per post first.

What is a good engagement rate?

It depends on the platform and niche, but 1 to 3 percent is often considered average and above 3 percent good. Use the result as a benchmark, not a verdict.

Where do I find average view duration?

In YouTube Studio analytics, on the Reach or Engagement tab of a video. Instagram and TikTok show average watch time in their insights for video posts.

Is my data stored?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

What is audience retention on YouTube?

Retention shows how much of a video people watch on average. Divide the average view duration by the video length and multiply by 100 to get a percentage.

Is engagement rate the same on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube?

The idea is the same: interactions compared with audience size. Platforms count different actions and some use views instead of followers, so compare accounts using the same method.
