The one formula behind the number

Engagement rate here means the average number of interactions per post, divided by your audience size, as a percentage. You enter followers (or views, if you prefer to measure that way), how many posts you are looking at, and the totals for likes, comments, shares and saves across those posts. The calculator adds the interactions, divides by the number of posts, divides again by the audience, and multiplies by 100.

Take an account with 8,000 followers. Over 12 posts it collected 1,800 likes, 120 comments, 60 shares and 180 saves. That is 2,160 interactions, or 180 per post, and 180 divided by 8,000 is 2.25 percent. The tool shows this working under the result so you can check the arithmetic.

Followers or views?

Measuring by followers tells you how much of your audience reacts to a typical post. Measuring by views tells you how engaging the post was for the people who actually saw it, which is the fairer test for video, where reach is often far above or below follower count. Pick the one that matches the way you want to compare, and stick with it so the numbers are comparable from month to month.

How to read the rating

The tool labels a rate under 1 percent as low, 1 to 3 percent as average, 3 to 6 percent as good and above 6 percent as excellent. Those bands are a rule of thumb, not an official figure from any platform. Small accounts tend to see higher rates than large ones, and a reel does not behave like a carousel, so compare yourself with similar accounts and with your own history before you decide anything.

Video retention

The second calculator takes the length of a video and the average view duration from your analytics and gives the average retention. A 10-minute video with an average view of 4 minutes 30 seconds retains 45 percent. You can type times as mm:ss, hh:mm:ss or plain seconds. If your retention drops sharply in the first few seconds, the opening needs work; if it falls steadily, the video may simply be longer than the idea.

Questions people ask

What is a good engagement rate on Instagram?

Many creators aim for somewhere between 1 and 3 percent, and anything above 3 percent is generally considered strong. Numbers vary a lot by niche and account size, so use them as a loose guide.

Should I use followers or views?

Use followers to see how your audience responds overall. Use views when you want to judge a single piece of content, especially video.

Where do I find the numbers I need?

Your platform's analytics or insights page lists likes, comments, shares, saves and average view duration for each post or video.

Why is my rate lower than my friend's?

Audience size, niche, posting frequency and format all change the rate. Larger audiences nearly always show lower percentages.

Does the calculator store my data?

No. The numbers stay in your browser.
