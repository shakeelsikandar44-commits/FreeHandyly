How to Use the Social Media Image Resizer on FreeHandyly

The FreeHandyly Social Media Image Resizer fits any picture to the exact pixel dimensions a platform expects. Choose a preset such as a YouTube thumbnail or an Instagram portrait post, decide how the image should fit, and download a sharp file. The work happens inside your browser, so your photos are never uploaded.

  1. Add an image. Click the upload area or drag a JPG, PNG, WebP or GIF file onto it. A live preview appears immediately.
  2. Choose a size preset. Pick a platform preset such as YouTube thumbnail 1280 x 720, Instagram square 1080 x 1080, Instagram portrait 1080 x 1350 or vertical 1080 x 1920 for Stories, Reels, TikTok and Shorts. Select Custom to enter your own width and height.
  3. Select a fit mode. Fill and crop covers the whole frame and trims the edges, Fit inside keeps the entire image and adds a background colour, and Stretch forces the exact size even if it distorts the picture.
  4. Adjust the crop position. When the image is cropped, use the horizontal and vertical sliders to decide which part stays in view, for example keeping a face near the top.
  5. Pick a format and download. Choose PNG for sharp graphics, JPG for photos or WebP for smaller files, set the quality, and press Download.

Why This Tool is Helpful

Every platform crops and compresses images differently. A picture with the wrong proportions can have faces cut off, text trimmed or blurry edges added when it is uploaded. Preparing the image at the right size first lets you control what viewers see, keeps text readable and avoids the heavy recompression that platforms apply to oversized files.

  • YouTubers can export thumbnails at 1280 x 720 pixels, the size YouTube recommends, and keep them under the file limit.
  • Instagram and TikTok creators can prepare square, portrait and vertical images without opening a design app.
  • Online sellers can standardise product photos for shops and marketplaces.
  • Marketing teams can adapt one visual to several channels in minutes.
  • Students and bloggers can create article headers and social previews with the right proportions.

Tips for sharper results

Start from the largest original you have, because enlarging a small image makes it look soft. Keep important text and faces away from the edges, since feeds, profile grids and video overlays can hide the outer part of an image. Use Fit inside with a matching background colour when you cannot afford to lose any content, and use PNG when the picture contains small text. Platform recommendations change over time, so check the official help pages if exact limits matter for a campaign.

Common image size mistakes

Many creators upload an image that is almost the right shape and let the platform crop it automatically, which is how faces end up cut off and captions disappear. Another frequent mistake is exporting a small image and stretching it, which produces soft edges. Decide the final size first, keep your subject in the middle third of the frame, and leave a margin around any text. For video thumbnails, remember that a timestamp badge covers the bottom right corner, and for Stories and Reels, interface buttons cover the top and bottom of the screen, so keep key content in the safe middle area. Preview the result at a small size to confirm that it is still readable on a phone.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. The image is processed locally in your browser with the canvas feature, so it never leaves your device.

What is the best size for a YouTube thumbnail?

YouTube recommends 1280 x 720 pixels, a 16:9 ratio, under 2 MB. Use the YouTube Thumbnail preset and choose JPG or WebP if the file is too large.

Why does my image look cropped?

Fill and crop scales the image to cover the whole frame, which trims whatever does not fit. Choose Fit inside to keep the entire image, or move the position sliders.

What are the maximum dimensions?

Custom sizes can be up to 8000 pixels on each side. Very large canvases may fail on older phones because of browser memory limits.

What size should an Instagram post be?

Common sizes are 1080 x 1080 pixels for square posts and 1080 x 1350 pixels for portrait posts. Choose the matching preset and the tool sets the exact pixels for you.

How do I resize an image without stretching it?

Choose a fit option that keeps the original proportions. Cropping fills the whole frame, while fitting inside keeps the whole picture and may leave empty space around it.
